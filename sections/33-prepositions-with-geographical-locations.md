# Prepositions with Geographical Locations

**PREPOSITIONS WITH GEOGRAPHICAL LOCATIONS**

Most locations whose names end in –e are feminine. Normally, names ending in any other letter are masculine (exception: le Mexique)

1. Countries, continents & states  
1. En is used to express the ideas of to, at, or in with the names of feminine countries, continents, and states.  
   

   Je vais en France.		*I’m going to France.*  
   Sont-ils en Asie.		*Are they in Asia?*  
   1’habite en Virginie.		*I live in Virginia.*  
     
2. à \+ definite article (to, at, or in) is used for masculine geographical locations.  
   

   Il va au Portugal.		*He’s going to Portugal.*  
   Vont-ils aux Etats-Unis?	*Are they going to the USA?*  
   Vous allez au Texas?		*You’re going to Texas.*

**de** is used without an article to express the idea of *from* wirh feminine countries, continents, and states; whereas **de** \+ definite article is used with masculine countries, continents, and states.

Cities   
1\. **à** is always used with cities to express the isea of  *to, at, & in.* Almost all cities are neither masculine or feminine, and thus, will never be preceded by an article.

| Il est à New York?  Non, il est à Paris/ | Is he in NewYork? No, he’s in Paris.  |
| :---- | :---- |

| \*Le Mardi gras est à la Nouvelle Orléans.  \*Je vais au Havre. ( \= à \+ le)  | *The Mardi Gras is in New Orleans.* *I’m going to Le Havre.*  |
| :---- | :---- |

\*NOTE- There are a few cities that contain the definite article as part of its name; and thus, it must be retained. 

2\. **de** is used with cities to express the idea of **from**
