# Relative Pronouns

**RELATIVE PRONOUNS**

Relative pronouns join two clauses into a single sentence. A relative pronoun refers back to a word in the main clause and introduces a subordinate (dependent) clause. The form of the relative pronoun is determined by the grammatical function in the subordinate clause.

77
A. QUI functions as the *subject* of the second clause and is always followed by a verb. It is equivalent to the English *who, which,* or *that* and can refer back to either persons or things.

-Connais-tu M. Penaud?  
-C’est un prof qui enseigne l’anglais.

-Est-ce que tu aimes écrire des dissertations?  
-Oui. Mais je n’aime pas les dissertations qui prennent trop de temps.

B. QUE is always the object of the second clause and is followed by a subject and a verb. It is equivalent to *whom, which,* or *that* and can also refer to both persons and things. The final **-e** of QUE drops when followed by a vowel or vowel sound (**qu’**).

-Est-ce que vous avez aimé ce cours? *Did you like this course?*  
-Oui. Mais je n’ai pas aimé les dissertations **que** nous avons **faites** en classe. *Yes, but I didn’t like the compositions that we did in class.*

-Comment trouve-t-elle ce prof? *What does she think of that teacher?*  
-C’est un prof **qu**’elle aime. *He’s a teacher whom she likes.*
